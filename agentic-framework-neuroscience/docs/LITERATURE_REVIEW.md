# Literature review: small-group behavior in human–AI and agent teams

**Scope.** Papers from CHI, CSCW (PACM HCI), UIST, and AAAI, published between late 2022 and October 2026, that study groups where at least three humans or agents act together. Most studies use triads plus an AI (4 actors). The largest is a five-person group plus a facilitator (6 actors). Two works from outside the target venues are listed separately because they directly shape the memory design.

**Anchor paper.** Shao, Zhao, Padmakumar, Wang & Yang, *Human–AI Collaboration at Scale: Task Criticality, Agency, and Friction across 250,000 Conversations* (Stanford, 2026). It studies one human with one AI. The papers below cover what happens when several such pairs work together.

## Papers reviewed

| # | Paper | Venue | Group setting | Key finding | What it means for this framework |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chiang, Lu, Li & Yin. *Are Two Heads Better Than One in AI-Assisted Decision Making?* | CHI 2023 | Groups of 3 humans + 1 AI recommender, compared with individuals | Groups relied on the AI **more** than individuals did, even when it was wrong. They were more confident when they overturned it, and they made fairer decisions. | Being in a group does not reduce over-reliance by itself. Agent output needs visible provenance and a review step. |
| 2 | Schelble, Flathmann, Musick, McNeese & Freeman. *I See You: Examining the Role of Spatial Information in Human-Agent Teams* | CSCW 2022 (PACM HCI 6, CSCW2) | 3-member teams: H-H-H, H-H-A, and H-A-A (NeoCITIES) | Teams with more agents performed best. Shared awareness ("spatial information") sped up team cognition and helped people understand the agents. | Shared state should be visible to everyone, not hidden inside the agents. This is why global memory is a board the humans can see. |
| 3 | Liu, Fang, Shi, Wu, Igarashi & Chen. *Proactive Conversational Agents with Inner Thoughts* | CHI 2025 | Multi-party text chat; formative study of 24 people in group chats; three-agent demos | Next-speaker prediction is not enough. Agents should form covert thoughts, rate their own motivation to speak, and wait for the right moment. Participants preferred this approach in over 82% of comparisons. | Each agent scores its motivation to speak, and only the strongest solo agent takes the floor on a given turn. |
| 4 | Doherty, Perkoff, von Bayern, Zhang, Dey, Bodzianowski, Puntambekar & Hirshfield. *Piecing Together Teamwork: A Responsible Approach to an LLM-based Educational Jigsaw Agent* | CHI 2025 | 58 dyads/triads of students (ages 12–17) + 1 agent, with a human in the loop | The agent reduced social loafing and increased respectful collaboration. The human in the loop **accepted, rejected, ignored, or modified** agent messages before they were sent. | This is the review-before-send pattern used for drafts in "Human + agent" mode (send as-is / edit / discard). |
| 5 | Chen, Yuan, Zhang, Zheng & Wei. *Maintaining "Balanced" Conflict: Proactive Intervention Strategies of AI Voice Agents in Online Collaboration of Temporary Design Teams* | CHI 2025 | Teams of 3 designers + 1 AI voice agent (N=36) | Teams need a moderate level of disagreement. The agent played devil's advocate when consensus formed too fast and voiced opinions members held back. | This is the basis for the **Critic** role: it reacts to consensus language ("agree", "final", "lock in"). |
| 6 | Cai, Park, Nixon & Doroudi. *Advancing Knowledge Together: Integrating LLM-based Conversational AI in Small Group Collaborative Learning* | CHI EA 2024 | 3 students + 1 LLM facilitator | The timing and role of interventions mattered most, and students showed signs of over-relying on the bot. | Each pair's teaching-mode toggle supports learning rather than offloading. The Facilitator rate-limits itself. |
| 7 | Alsobay, Rothschild, Hofman & Goldstein. *Bringing Everyone to the Table: An Experimental Study of LLM-Facilitated Group Decision Making* | CSCW 2026 (PACM HCI 10, CSCW059) | 281 groups of 5 humans + 1 GPT-4o or human facilitator (1,475 participants), hidden-profile task | LLM facilitation increased information sharing by lifting the least-engaged members. Final decisions still did not improve, because the hidden-profile bias was stronger. | The Facilitator invites the quietest pair. The hidden-profile preset tests whether private facts make it into shared memory. Information sharing alone isn't enough, so the Analyst also brings each pair's private evidence forward. |
| 8 | Park, O'Brien, Cai, Morris, Liang & Bernstein. *Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior* | UIST 2023 | 25 interacting agents | A memory stream, reflection, and retrieval are what make agents behave believably over long interactions. | Memory is a first-class component. Items are typed (decision / plan / fact / question) so agents retrieve structure, not just transcript. |
| 9 | Yen & Zhao. *Memolet: Reifying the Reuse of User-AI Conversational Memories* | UIST 2024 | Single user (N=12) | Users want to directly see and choose which memories an AI reuses. | Pair memory is editable and visible. Humans decide what gets shared. |
| 10 | Vaithilingam et al. *SemanticCommit: Helping Users Update Intent Specifications for AI Memory at Scale* | UIST 2025 | Single user (N=12) | Updating memory needs conflict detection and a human decision. Users valued checking retrieval separately from generation. | Agent decisions on consequential and high-stakes tasks go to **sign-off** instead of being written straight to shared memory. |
| 11 | Hu, Chen, Li, Phadnis, Xu, Qian, Olwal, Kim, Heo & Du. *DialogLab: Authoring, Simulating, and Testing Dynamic Human-AI Group Conversations* | UIST 2025 | Authoring tool for multi-party conversations mixing humans and AI | Multi-party designs need a runtime with both real and simulated humans, plus analysis of group dynamics afterward. | The offline agents plus "Run agent round" let you rehearse a session. The observer view and JSON export support analysis afterward. |
| 12 | Zhang et al. *ProAgent: Building Proactive Cooperative Agents with Large Language Models* | AAAI 2024 | LLM agents cooperating with AI and human-proxy partners (Overcooked-AI) | Agents that infer teammates' intentions and revise their beliefs coordinate better in zero-shot settings. | Each agent's context includes a team summary (roles, modes, contribution counts) so it can model the other pairs. |

**Outside the target venues, but directly relevant:**

- Chiang, Lu, Li & Yin, *Enhancing AI-Assisted Group Decision Making through LLM-Powered Devil's Advocate* (IUI 2024). A devil's advocate that argued **against the AI** and responded dynamically improved groups' appropriate reliance on the AI. One that argued against the majority had little effect. This is why the Critic also challenges the other agents, not only the humans.
- Rezazadeh et al., *Collaborative Memory: Multi-User Memory Sharing in LLM Agents with Dynamic Access Control* (ICML 2025). It proposes two memory tiers, private and shared, where every fragment carries provenance. This is the closest technical precedent for the global and pair memory split used here.

## Themes

**1. Groups don't fix over-reliance on their own.** Groups lean on AI more than individuals (paper 1). Facilitation increases information sharing without fixing decisions (paper 7). Students drifted toward over-relying on a group bot (paper 6). The anchor paper finds the same pattern for one person with one AI: in the highest-stakes tier, adapting and critiquing AI output decline while simply understanding it becomes more common. The authors warn this "might lead to the weakening of human oversight precisely where it matters most." The framework responds with provenance on every memory item, review before an agent speaks for a pair, and human sign-off on high-stakes decisions.

**2. When an agent speaks matters as much as what it says.** Proactive turn-taking based on the agent's own motivation (paper 3), well-timed interventions (papers 5 and 6), and inviting the least-engaged members (paper 7) all favor agents that sometimes stay silent. The framework rates each agent's motivation against a threshold, lets only one solo agent take a turn, and caps agent-to-agent chains at two turns.

**3. Productive conflict is a design target.** Moderate disagreement helps (paper 5). A devil's advocate aimed at the AI improves reliance (IUI 2024). The anchor paper finds that friction is "productive" mostly in high-stakes, non-routine work. The framework includes a Critic agent and lets humans flag friction. It then logs how the human recovers: repair, reformulate, or ask a question.

**4. Shared memory has to be visible and governed.** Shared awareness builds team cognition (paper 2). Memory works better when it's structured (paper 8), when users can choose what gets reused (paper 9), and when updates go through human review (paper 10). The framework separates global memory from private pair memory, uses typed items with provenance, lets humans explicitly share private notes, and requires sign-off.

**5. Human oversight should be adjustable per task.** The jigsaw agent's human in the loop (paper 4) and the anchor paper's finding that agency is a personal choice rather than a property of the task together suggest letting each pair set its own level of involvement. The three participation modes map onto the Human Agency Scale: Agent only ≈ H1–H2, Human + agent ≈ H3–H4, Human only = H5.

## Gaps this framework is built to study

- **Asymmetric pairs.** None of the reviewed studies let each sub-team pick its own level of AI involvement within the same task. For example, one pair could join with its agent only while the other two keep their humans in.
- **Two-tier memory with humans in charge.** The Collaborative Memory paper formalizes private and shared tiers for agents, but no HCI study puts that control in the hands of the human members of a mixed team.
- **Measuring the anchor paper's facets inside groups.** The system logs engagement mode, friction source and recovery, and estimated agency per pair, so findings from one-on-one chats can be tested in a group setting.
